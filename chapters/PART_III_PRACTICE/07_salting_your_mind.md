# Chapter 7: Salting Your Mind

## Where Energy Hits First

Your mind is where negativity, toxicity, and spiritual decay hit you first. Before it affects your home or your relationships or your life, it affects your thoughts.

When you wake up, what's the first thing you think about? Is it worry? Comparison? Resentment? Dread? If you haven't salted your mind, decay has already begun.

This is why **Colossians 4:6** says: "Let your conversation be always full of grace, seasoned with salt. Know how to answer everyone."

Notice: It starts with conversation. It starts with *what you say*. And what you say begins with what you think. If your mind is unsalted, full of rot and decay, your words will be too. But if your mind is salted—preserved, seasoned, clear—then your words will flavor the world around you.

---

## Daily Salting: Morning

The most crucial time to salt your mind is **first thing in the morning**, before anything else reaches you.

Your mind is most open in the morning. You haven't yet been bombarded by news, messages, other people's emotions, or the day's demands. This is when you're most able to set the tone for your saltiness.

### Morning Salting Practice (10-15 minutes)

**Step 1: Ground yourself in stillness**
- Before reaching for your phone, coffee, or plans, sit still for 2-3 minutes
- Focus on your breath. Feel your body. Notice the quiet
- You're establishing a baseline of presence

**Step 2: Set your salt intention**
- Ask yourself: What kind of salt do I want to be today?
- What do I want to preserve? What do I want to flavor?
- What toxins or decay am I likely to encounter, and how do I want to respond?

Not as fixing-it-all sponge, but as seasoning—bringing grace, clarity, wisdom.

**Step 3: Name three things**
- Name three specific things you're grateful for *right now*
- Not big, lofty things necessarily—your coffee, the light, a person's kindness
- Gratitude seasons your mind. It's a preservative against bitterness and resentment

**Step 4: Return to a core truth**
- Recite or read something that reminds you of who you are and why you're here
- This might be Scripture: Colossians 4:6, Matthew 5:13, a Psalm that centers you
- Or it might be a simple statement: "I bring salt to today. My presence matters. I preserve what's good."

**Step 5: Taste the salt**
- Drink something. Eat something. Let your body experience salt.
- This might sound strange, but physical sensation anchors the practice
- You're not just thinking about saltiness; you're tasting it

## During the Day: Filtering

Throughout the day, your mind will encounter toxins: gossip, comparison, despair, anxiety, resentment, envy. These are the spiritual equivalent of what causes decay. And your job is to **filter them out**.

### Toxins That Cause Mental Decay

**Gossip** — Talking negatively about others, passing on information to damage reputation or create drama. Gossip is like poison in meat; it causes decay in relationships.

**Comparison** — Measuring yourself against others and finding yourself wanting. Comparison rots your sense of self-worth and purpose.

**Catastrophizing** — Taking one small problem and spiraling it into disaster. This decays your trust and creates false decay where none exists.

**Resentment** — Holding onto wrongs done to you, replaying them, building a case. Resentment rots joy and prevents healing.

**Envy** — Desiring what others have and resenting that you don't. Envy decays gratitude and contentment.

**Anxiety about tomorrow** — Borrowing problems from the future instead of staying present. This creates decay in your today.

### The Filter Practice

When you notice one of these toxins entering your mind:

**1. Pause.** Actually stop what you're doing for a moment. Don't push forward into the toxic thought.

**2. Name it.** Be specific. "This is gossip I'm engaging in" or "This is comparison" or "This is catastrophizing." Naming it creates distance.

**3. Ask: Is this salt or rot?** Would entertaining this thought preserve anything good? Would it flavor my life with grace? Or is it decay? Be honest.

**4. Release it.** Don't engage further. Don't analyze it. Don't try to understand why you're thinking it. Simply let it pass. 

You might say: "I'm releasing this. Not today. Not my salt."

**5. Return to one true thing.** Replace the decay with something salt-like. Something true, something preserving.

Examples:
- From catastrophizing, return to: "Today is okay. Right now is okay. I'm safe."
- From comparison, return to: "My path is mine. I have what I need for today."
- From gossip-urge, return to: "I preserve others' dignity, even when tempted not to."
- From resentment, return to: "I release the past. I choose to preserve my peace."

## Media & Information: Selective Salting

In the modern world, a huge source of mental decay is **the constant stream of negative information**. News, social media, even some podcasts and shows are designed to trigger anxiety, outrage, and comparison.

This isn't moralism about media. It's recognizing that **what you consume shapes what your mind becomes**.

If you start the day salting your mind with gratitude and center, then spend three hours scrolling doomscroll media and comparison traps, you've unraveled your salt. You've let the rot back in.

### Media Salting Practice

**Be intentional, not reactive.**
- Don't doom-scroll first thing in the morning
- Don't check news or social media while eating or alone
- Don't consume media right before bed
- Don't fall into infinite scroll that's designed to addict you

**Curate your feed.**
- Follow people who inspire you, not compare-trap you
- Read news from sources that inform, not inflame
- Watch shows that expand you, not deplete you
- Listen to podcasts that salt your mind, not decay it

**Set boundaries.**
- Choose specific times for media, not constant access
- Use app timers if you need external help
- Leave your phone in another room during meals, work, and conversations
- Notice when you're using media to avoid feeling something—that's a sign to pause

**Ask the salting question:**
Before consuming any media, ask: "Will this preserve something good in me? Will this flavor my mind with clarity or peace? Or is this decay?"

Sometimes decay is necessary—staying informed about real problems, for instance. But staying informed isn't the same as staying afraid. You can know what's happening without absorbing the panic around it. You can be aware without being addicted to outrage.

## Evening Salting

If morning salts your mind with intention, evening salts it with **integration and release**.

### Evening Salting Practice (10 minutes)

**Step 1: Reflect on the day**
- What happened today? Specifically, where did you bring salt—preserve something, flavor something, change something with your presence?
- Where did you lose saltiness—absorb too much, become like everything else, let decay in?
- Be honest but not harsh. You're noticing, not condemning.

**Step 2: Release what you're carrying**
- Think about the day's heaviness, conversations, conflicts, worries
- Imagine them as water that you're letting flow away from you
- You don't carry the day with you into sleep
- One way to do this: write down what you're releasing, then burn the paper (safely) or trash it

**Step 3: Name one good thing**
- Something you did that was salt-like, or something that happened that preserved goodness
- Even a small moment counts: a kind word, a boundary held, a worry released, a presence offered

**Step 4: Set your night intention**
- "I sleep in peace. My mind is preserved. Tomorrow I salt again."
- A simple statement that returns you to your saltiness as you rest

## Salting Your Self-Talk

One of the most important dimensions of salting your mind is **what you say to yourself**.

Your inner voice—the running commentary in your head—is incredibly powerful. If your self-talk is laced with shame, criticism, anxiety, and self-doubt, you're constantly decaying from the inside.

### Unsalty Self-Talk
- "I'm not good enough."
- "I always mess up."
- "People don't like me."
- "I can't do this."
- "I'm falling apart."
- "I'm a fraud."
- "I'm too much or not enough."

Each of these is decay. Each corrodes your sense of self and your ability to function as salt.

### Salty Self-Talk
- "I'm doing my best with what I know."
- "This is hard, and I can handle hard."
- "My presence matters."
- "I'm learning."
- "I'm worthy as I am."
- "I belong here."
- "My saltiness is real and enough."

This isn't toxic positivity or denying real struggles. It's choosing to speak to yourself with the same grace and preservation you'd offer to someone you love.

### Practicing Salt-Like Self-Talk

**Notice your patterns.** For one day, just listen to your inner voice. Don't change it, just notice. What themes emerge? What accusations or fears repeat?

**Get curious.** Where did this voice come from? Who told you these things? Is this even true, or is it a story you absorbed?

**Replace it intentionally.** When you notice unsalty self-talk, pause. Say something different. Say something salt-like. Not fake, but true and preserving.

**Give yourself time.** Your inner voice has likely been negative for years. Changing it takes practice. Be patient with yourself.

## When Your Mind Gets Stale

Even with daily salting, sometimes your mind feels stale, flat, unsalty. This happens. You're tired. You've absorbed too much. You've let decay in. Your mind feels like old, damp bread—not fresh, not preserving anything, just... stale.

When this happens, you need a **reset**.

### Mind Reset Practice (2-3 hours, or a full day)

**Step 1: Stop consuming.** Put the phone away. No news, no social media, no podcasts, no stimulation. Your mind is overwhelmed.

**Step 2: Move your body.** Walk, dance, stretch, exercise. Movement helps discharge the staleness and reconnect you to aliveness.

**Step 3: Create something.** Write, cook, draw, plant something. Creation is the opposite of decay. It reminds you that you're not just absorbing; you're generative.

**Step 4: Be in nature.** Sit outside. Breathe fresh air. Put your hands in soil or water. Nature salts the mind—preserves what's real and living.

**Step 5: Connect with real people.** Not in a draining way, but genuinely. Talk to someone who makes you feel more alive. Share a meal. Laugh.

**Step 6: Rest.** Let your mind be still. Sleep deeply. Stop trying to improve yourself for a day.

After a reset like this, your mind will feel salted again—fresh, preserved, ready to flavor the world.

## Salting Your Mind in Crisis

Sometimes your mind encounters crisis—loss, betrayal, fear, trauma. In these moments, salting isn't about maintaining positivity. It's about **preserving yourself through the hardship**.

In crisis, salting your mind means:
- Telling the truth about what happened
- Feeling what needs to be felt (grief, anger, fear)
- But doing so without letting decay take over
- Seeking help—therapy, spiritual direction, trusted friends
- Returning repeatedly to what's real and solid in you
- Not absorbing the crisis as your identity

You're not salting over the pain or pretending it's not there. You're preserving yourself *through* the pain so that you can eventually move through it.
