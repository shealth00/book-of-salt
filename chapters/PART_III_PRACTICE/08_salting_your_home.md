# Chapter 8: Salting Your Home

## Why Your Home Matters

Your home is sacred space. Not in a religious-only sense, but literally—a space set apart, distinct from the chaos outside. It's where you rest, where you're most vulnerable, where the people you love gather (or where you gather with yourself).

And like any sacred space, your home can become stale. Heavy. Stagnant. It can accumulate the residue of conflicts, illness, stress, and just the general decay of unattended space. When this happens, your home stops preserving you. Instead, you're absorbing the heaviness every time you walk through the door.

Salting your home means **actively creating and maintaining a space that preserves and flavors, rather than decays**.

This isn't about perfectionism or aesthetics. It's not about Pinterest-worthy homes. It's about functional preservation—making your space work *for* you, not against you.

---

## Physical Salting: The Ritual

Let's start with the practical salt ritual that many traditions use. This is grounded in understanding salt as a preserving, purifying substance.

### The Physical Salt Ritual (1-2 hours)

**What you need:**
- Sea salt (preferred, though any salt works)
- Small bowls or dishes
- Intention and attention
- Optional: essential oils, herbs, or other natural elements to add to the salt

**The practice:**

**Step 1: Set intention**
- Before you begin, get still for a moment
- What do you want to clear from your home? Heavy emotions? Stagnant energy? Conflict?
- What do you want to preserve? Peace? Clarity? Connection?
- Speak this intention aloud or in writing

**Step 2: Place salt in corners and thresholds**
- Go through your home deliberately
- Place bowls or small piles of salt in the four corners of each room
- Place salt on windowsills
- Place salt at doorways and thresholds—the boundaries between spaces
- Corners and thresholds are where stagnant energy gets trapped; salt releases it

**Step 3: Let it sit**
- Leave the salt out for 24-48 hours
- During this time, notice how your home *feels*
- Does it seem lighter? Clearer? More settled?
- You might find yourself sleeping better, feeling calmer, or being more peaceful

**Step 4: Dispose respectfully**
- After 24-48 hours, gather the salt
- Never consume this salt; you don't want to take back what you've cleared
- Dispose of it outside your home, away from where you regularly walk
- Some traditions suggest running water over it or burying it; others sweep it into a trash or compost
- The act of removing it completes the clearing

**Step 5: Return to stillness**
- Once the salt is cleared, sit in your home again
- Notice the difference
- Feel the clearer space
- Anchor this feeling so you remember what preserved space feels like

## When to Salt Your Home

You don't need to do a full salt ritual constantly. But there are specific times when it becomes important:

**Monthly:** Once a month during a regular cleaning cycle is good maintenance. It keeps your space from accumulating too much stagnant energy.

**After conflict:** If there's been a major argument, breakdown, or heavy conversation in your home, salt afterward to clear the residue.

**After illness:** When someone's been sick in your home, salt helps clear the heaviness and stagnation.

**When something feels off:** Sometimes a space just feels heavy or wrong, even if you can't name why. That's a sign to salt.

**Seasonally:** Many traditions salt homes at the change of seasons—spring cleaning with salt, autumn clearing with salt. This marks the transition and keeps the home fresh.

**After a guest leaves:** Especially if someone difficult or draining has been in your home, salt clears their residue.

**When you move in:** If you're new to a space, salting before you settle in clears whatever energy was there before and sets the tone for your own presence.

## Salting Your Home Through Cleanliness

Salt rituals are powerful, but they work best alongside **actual, intentional cleanliness**.

Decay doesn't just live in "energy"—it lives in physical dirt, clutter, and disorder. A cluttered home doesn't preserve peace; it creates stress. A dirty home accumulates actual bacteria and mold, not just spiritual staleness.

So salting your home includes:

**Regular cleaning**
- Not obsessive, not perfectionistic
- But intentional and consistent
- A weekly reset where you clean floors, surfaces, bathrooms
- This keeps decay from accumulating

**Decluttering**
- Things you don't use or love don't preserve your space; they clutter it
- Regularly go through your home and remove what's not serving you
- This isn't minimalism; it's keeping what you actually use and love, removing what's excess

**Opening windows and airing out**
- Let fresh air move through your home
- Stagnant air is literally dead air
- Fresh air salts your space better than anything

**Clearing out the old**
- When seasons change, clear out old clothes, expired food, things that belong to past versions of your life
- Make space for new

**Caring for what you keep**
- Make sure things in your home actually work
- Fix what's broken rather than letting it sit
- Polish what needs polishing
- A functioning home preserves; a broken home decays

## Creating Sacred Corners

Beyond the salt ritual, you can create specific spaces in your home that are *intentionally* preserving and peaceful.

### A Meditation or Prayer Corner
- Choose a quiet spot, even if it's just a corner of your bedroom
- Place a comfortable cushion or chair
- Add something beautiful: a plant, a candle, a meaningful object
- This becomes your salting space—where you return to your saltiness daily

### A Grounding Spot
- An altar-like space (doesn't need to be religious) where you place things that ground you
- Might include: a photo of someone you love, a meaningful object, a stone from somewhere important, a candle
- You return here when you're ungrounded, feeling unsalty, needing to remember who you are

### A Cleansing Space
- The bathroom can be a sacred space—somewhere you wash away the day
- Make it intentional: good soap, a candle, perhaps music or intention-setting
- The act of washing becomes salting

### A Gathering Space
- Whether it's a dining table or a living room, create a space where people come together
- Keep it clear and welcoming
- Make it a place where real connection happens, not where everyone hides on phones
- This space naturally preserves relationships and connection

## Salting Through Intention-Setting

Beyond the physical salt and cleanliness, salting your home is about **intentionality**—actively choosing what energy and presence you want in your space.

**Morning home blessing**
- As you move through your home in the morning, bless it
- "May this home preserve peace. May those who enter here feel safe. May decay not take root here."
- Simple, but it sets the tone

**Evening gratitude walk**
- Before bed, walk through your home and notice what you're grateful for
- The people/solitude in this space, the shelter, the safety, the things you use
- Gratitude salts a space

**Threshold intention**
- Every time you enter your home, pause at the door
- Feel the transition from outside chaos to inside peace
- Say something simple: "I return to my salt. This is safe space."

**Hosting with salt**
- When people come to your home, your presence should season it, not absorb
- You're creating preservation, not holding their heaviness
- You're bringing your clarity and peace into the gathering, not taking on their chaos

## When Your Home Has Heavy History

Sometimes a space carries heaviness from the past—previous occupants' trauma, conflict that happened there, grief or loss that seeped into the walls.

Salt ritual is helpful, but you might also need:

**Speaking to the space**
- Acknowledge what happened: "I know grief lived here. I acknowledge it. Now I'm choosing peace."
- This isn't denying the past; it's honoring it while not being controlled by it

**Writing on paper and burning**
- Write what you want to clear: "I release this conflict. I clear this heaviness."
- Safely burn the paper as a symbolic release
- The salt ritual can follow

**Blessing the space actively**
- Literally walk through your home and bless it aloud
- "I fill this space with light. I preserve peace here."
- Say it like you mean it—with presence and intention

**Professional help**
- If the heaviness feels really significant, spiritual direction, therapy, or even hiring someone to help energetically clear the space might be worthwhile
- There's no shame in getting help to clear deeply rooted heaviness

## Salting Shared Spaces

If you live with family, roommates, or a partner, salting your home gets more complex. You can't force others to participate.

**What you can do:**
- Salt your own bedroom or personal space
- Create shared rituals (monthly cleaning, opening windows, a family gratitude practice)
- Bring your salt—your presence, peace, boundaries—into shared spaces
- Let your presence season the space

**What you can't do:**
- Force others to understand or participate
- Control how others treat the space
- Absorb responsibility for others' contributions to decay

The goal is to bring salt where you can, accept what you can't control, and remember that your presence matters even if others don't understand the practice.

## Salting Digitally

Your digital space is a home too—email, phone, computer, social media.

**Digital salting includes:**

**Organizing your digital space**
- Clear email clutter regularly
- Organize files so you know where things are
- Don't let digital stuff pile up and become chaos

**Curating what enters your space**
- Unsubscribe from things that drain you
- Mute or block people who bring negativity
- Follow accounts that inspire and preserve

**Taking breaks from your devices**
- Your digital home shouldn't be your only home
- Regular unplugging preserves your actual, physical space

**Speaking intentionally online**
- Your words in digital space still season or decay
- Write emails with care, respond with grace, choose kindness
- Digital spaces need salt too

## Maintaining Your Salt

After you've done the physical salt ritual and intentional work:

**Weekly:** A light reset—tidying, opening windows, resetting your sacred corner

**Monthly:** Deeper clean and another salt ritual or blessing

**Seasonally:** Major clearing, clearing clutter, resetting the space

**As needed:** When the space feels heavy or stagnant again, return to salt ritual

The key is that salting isn't a one-time event. It's an ongoing practice. Your home is alive; it needs consistent preservation.

## Looking Ahead

Your mind is salted. Your home is preserved. Now we turn to perhaps the most challenging dimension of all: **salting your life**—your relationships, your choices, your boundaries, your presence in the world. This is where salt becomes most difficult to maintain, and where it's most needed.
